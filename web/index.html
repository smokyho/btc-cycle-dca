Current condition — multi-timeframe

Three views of the same market. Daily and weekly show the short-term picture; monthly is the slow view that decides the trend. RSI above 70 means stretched up, below 30 means stretched down. vs MA200 is how far price sits above or below its 200-period average. The interesting signal is disagreement between timeframes: when daily and weekly say "uptrend" while monthly still says "range", the short-term move is usually a rally inside a longer decline rather than a new trend — which is where the market is right now.

Where price sits against the forecast range

What this is, and what it is not. The two ranges combine independent methods: volatility bands (how far price could plausibly travel over a year if it moved randomly) and the cycle-amplitude model (how large each past halving rally turned out to be). Both are scenarios, not predictions. Only three halving cycles have ever completed, the volatility bands deliberately assume zero drift, and no method here can tell you when anything happens. Use the ranges as a planning band, not a target.

Halvings & historical cycles

A record, not a pattern. Each row is the window between one halving and the next. d A→high and d A→low count days from the start of that window. With only four halvings in Bitcoin's history there is not enough data to call any of this a reliable rule — it is a description of what happened four times.
